# Megascans-level ground and rock materials

The task: the coast lab's ground has no anti-tiling, and the "sand" reads as dirt. The target is ground and rock
surfaces at the level of Megascans, generated by code. This document finds out exactly how that is done and how
the kit can do all of it. It covers anti-tiling, layer blending, real-world scale, how sand reads, and one complete
technique for the landscape material and the cliff material.

Every claim carries its source: a `file:line` in this repository or in the engine (`C:\GameEngien\UE_5.8`), the
text of an engine asset (read with `strings`), a Poly Haven / ambientCG API response, or a paper that was read.
Anything that was not checked is listed under "Not verified" at the end.

## 1. The answer in one paragraph

Each ground layer is sampled at its real-world size and is hex-tiled. Histogram-preserving blending is used for
albedo, roughness, AO and height (Heitz & Neyret 2018, in Burley's 2019 per-channel form). Normals are blended as
derivatives, with a random per-tile rotation (Mikkelsen 2022). All maps of a layer share one lattice, one set of
offsets, rotations and weights. That keeps colour, normal and height aligned.

Layers meet by height-weighted blending. Every layer carries a real displacement map packed into the ARM texture.
The blend is built as a sum of `landscapeLayerWeight` terms, so absent layers stay compiled out per component. Near
and far use two scales of the same layer. A real-size aerial scan of the same coast (Poly Haven aerial sets are
15-30 m) multiplies the albedo as a macro layer, normalised by its own mean.

Sand gets a close-up set (about 2 m) instead of the aerial photo it has now. It also gets a wet band tied to the
water line. In that band albedo drops sharply, roughness drops, and a water film appears at saturation. Ripples are
part of the height/normal layer. Grain sparkle comes from UE's own Substrate glints.

The cliff stays world-projected, but with the engine's biplanar projection (2 projections, not 3), hex-tiled
inside each projection, at real size plus a macro scale. All of it is written as Custom HLSL nodes in the TSX
material system, using `SampleGrad` and the engine's shared wrap sampler.

## 4. Anti-tiling methods, with formulas and cost

### 4.1 Histogram-preserving stochastic tiling (Heitz & Neyret 2018)

- The output plane is cut into a triangle (simplex) grid. Each vertex gets a random patch of the input. A point is
  the blend of the 3 patches of its triangle, weighted by barycentrics (HPG 2018 abstract, PDF from HAL
  `hal-01824773`).
- Linear blending of independent samples convolves the histogram. That causes contrast loss, ghosting and new
  colours. Variance-preserving blending fixes the Gaussian case exactly: `G = (Σ wᵢ Gᵢ − μ)/√(Σ wᵢ²) + μ` (paper §3.2,
  "Exact histogram preservation in the Gaussian case").
- General inputs are first "Gaussianized" by a histogram transform `T`, which in the paper is a 3D optimal-transport
  solve. They are blended in Gaussian space, then mapped back by `T⁻¹`, stored as a 32³ LUT (§4, Table 1).
- Cost: 3 texture fetches plus 1 LUT fetch. It measured 0.29 ms at 1920×1080 on a GTX 980, or 0.11 ms with no LUT
  for Gaussian inputs (paper, figure captions and §6). Derivatives are taken before the random offsets and passed
  to `textureGrad` (§5).
- Limit: inputs with "a strong pattern-like organization or, worse, repetition" fail (Fig. 15). The target class is
  stochastic natural textures, "such as moss, granite, sand, bark".

### 4.2 Burley 2019 (JCGT 8(4))

- `T` is per-channel 1D LUTs built "trivially from the input histogram on texture load". That replaces the minutes of
  optimal transport (abstract).
- A truncated Gaussian plus a soft-clipping contrast operator removes the clipping artifacts of fixed-point storage
  (§3).
- Colouration: histogram preservation runs on luminance only, in YCbCr (§4).
- Exponentiated weights `wᵢ' = wᵢ^γ / Σ wⱼ^γ` reduce ghosting. In Fig. 10, ghosting shows at γ ≤ 2, tile
  structure at γ ≥ 8, and γ = 4 preserves structure (§5).

### 4.3 Deliot & Heitz 2019 (GPU Zen 2)

The authors' page (`eheitzresearch.wordpress.com/738-2/`) states two changes. The 3D transform becomes three 1D
transforms "in the eigenspace of the histogram", orders of magnitude faster. A LUT prefiltering step fixes the
colour drift at coarse mip levels. This is the implementation Mikkelsen built on (his §3).

### 4.4 Hex-tiling (Mikkelsen 2022, JCGT 11(2))

Read in full from the paper.

- It uses the same triangle lattice: `st *= 2√3`, skew by `float2x2(1, −0.57735027, 0, 1.15470054)`. Barycentrics
  `w1..w3` and integer vertices come from `TriangleGrid` (Listing 1).
- Each tile gets an offset `hash(vertex)` (Listing 2) and a rotation from `LoadRot2x2` (Listing 6) about its centre
  `MakeCenST` (Listing 5).
- It samples the source texture directly, with no histogram preservation. Weights follow Eq. 1:
  `Wᵢ = φ(xᵢ)·wᵢ^γ / Σ φ(xⱼ)·wⱼ^γ`, with γ = 7 and β = 0.6.
  - Normals: `φ_N = (1−β) + β·√(|d|²/(1+|d|²))`, the sine of the slope (Eq. 3).
  - Colour: `φ_C = (1−β) + β·luminance` (Eq. 4).
- Colour contrast is held by the gain curve `Gain3` (Eq. 5, Listing 8), with exponent `k = log(1−r)/log(0.5)`. The
  conservative range is `r ∈ [0.65, 0.75]`. Normals use `r = 0.5`, which is off (§4).
- Cost: 3 `SampleGrad` per map plus lattice math. Mikkelsen calls the quality trade-off for colour subjectively
  minor (§5). He shows normal maps "strikingly similar" to histogram-preserving (§4).

### 4.5 Texture bombing (Glanville, GPU Gems ch. 20)

- A regular cell grid. Each cell places the image at a random offset, decided by alpha and priority.
- It checks 9 neighbour cells in general, or 4 if the image stays within one cell and offsets are in [0,1].
- For rotation, the opaque part must fit a circle of diameter 1 in the cell.
- The random UV jumps at cell edges and breaks mip selection. Shaders can reach "hundreds of instructions".
- It fits decals and scattered objects, not a continuous ground surface.

### 4.6 UV distortion, multi-scale and macro masks

- **UV distortion** adds low-frequency noise to the UV. It moves features but does not change which texels appear,
  and it stretches features where the warp gradient is large. The derivatives of the warped UV feed the mip
  selection.
- **Multi-scale (near/far)** samples the same layer at two scales and blends by pixel distance. The far scale removes
  the repetition the near one shows at distance. The engine ships `MacroUVs` for it
  (`Engine/Content/Functions/Engine_MaterialFunctions02/MacroUVs.uasset`).
- **Macro variation** multiplies albedo by a large-scale signal. The engine ships `T_Default_MacroVariation`
  (`Engine/Content/EngineMaterials/T_Default_MacroVariation.uasset`) and Fab's `QMF_WorldspaceColorVariation`.

These three do not remove repetition at near range, because the same texels still repeat on a grid. They complement
4.1-4.4.

Best quality for stochastic ground (sand, gravel, grass ground) at 2-4 m tiles is the combination of 4.2 for
albedo, roughness, AO and height, and 4.4 for normals. All of these papers name sand and gravel as stochastic inputs.
It needs the precompute step. The kit owns the import pipeline (`vge/tools/coast-ground.mjs`), and Burley's
per-channel LUT is cheap to build there. Mikkelsen's direct-sampling form is what UE itself ships (section 5). It is
the fallback when a map has strong structure, where Heitz-Neyret fails (Fig. 15).

### 4.8 Normals under each method

- **Blend derivatives, not normals.** A tangent-space normal is the normal to a height field: `n = (−H_u, −H_v, 1)/√(…)`.
  The derivative is linear, so blending `(H_u, H_v)` equals blending the heights (Mikkelsen 2022 §2.1, Eq. 2,
  following Mikkelsen 2020 surface gradients). Convert with `TspaceNormalToDerivative` (Listing 9) and back with
  `normalize(float3(−d, 1))`.
- **Rotation.** With a per-tile rotation, sample at `mul(st−cen, rot)+cen+hash`, with gradients `mul(dSTdx, rot)`. Then
  rotate the derivative back: `d = mul(rot, d)` (Listing 3). This works only without histogram preservation on
  normals (§3).
- **Axis convention.** The rotation is in UV space, so the derivative must be in the UV frame first. The landscape
  tangent basis is `T = normalize(N.z, 0, −N.x)` and `B = cross(N, T)` (`Engine/Shaders/Private/LandscapeVertexFactory.ush:381-388`),
  which is world +X / +Y on flat ground. `LandscapeLayerCoords` XY mapping is also `TexCoord0 = local xy`
  (`MaterialExpressionLandscapeLayerCoords.cpp:56-59`), the same frame as the kit's `p.xy`. The texture's green
  convention, flipped at glTF import, sets the sign of `d.y` relative to `+v`. Mikkelsen's `gFlipVertDeriv`
  (Listing 9) exists for exactly this. The flag is set once from that convention, and the derivative is converted
  to the UV frame before rotation and back after.
- **UE TextureVariation** outputs the rotation in the B channel of its offset output, "used to fix up Normals". It
  ships `TextureVariation_RotateNormals` (asset text of `TextureVariation.uasset`). Megascans' `MF_NormalCorrection`
  says "Rotate Normal.xy with opposite rotation to fix normal angles" (asset text,
  `Engine/Plugins/Bridge/Content/MSPresets/M_MS_SurfaceBlend_Material/LayerFunctions/MF_NormalCorrection.uasset`).
- **Mip selection.** All of these need explicit gradients from the unshifted UV. Heitz-Neyret do this in §5,
  Mikkelsen in §3, and UE TextureVariation's pin text says "set your texture samples to MipValueMode: Derivative and
  use DDX, DDY from this Raw UVs pin".

## 6. Shared samplers inside Custom HLSL

For a texture sample with `SSM_Wrap_WorldGroupSettings`, the translator emits
`GetMaterialSharedSampler(<Tex>Sampler, View.MaterialTextureBilinearWrapedSampler)` and applies no manual mip bias.
The view's sampler already carries the world texture-group filter, anisotropy and the upscaler mip bias
(`HLSLMaterialTranslator.cpp:7357-7398`; `Engine/Source/Runtime/Renderer/Private/SceneRendering.cpp:1837-1863`).
`GetMaterialSharedSampler` returns the shared sampler when independent samplers are supported
(`Engine/Shaders/Private/MaterialTemplate.ush:2662-2665`).

A Custom node that samples its texture-object inputs should do exactly the same:
`T.SampleGrad(GetMaterialSharedSampler(TSampler, View.MaterialTextureBilinearWrapedSampler), uv, dx, dy)`. Then every
material texture shares one sampler state. Inverse LUTs use `View.MaterialTextureBilinearClampedSampler` the same
way (`:7376-7381`). Landscape weightmaps use `View.LandscapeWeightmapSampler` (`:7382-7385`, `:9064`, `:9070`).

## 7. Layer blending

### 7.1 What the engine does

- `LandscapeLayerWeight` is `base + layer·w`. If the layer is not painted on the component, the weight is
  `INDEX_NONE` and only `base` is compiled (`MaterialExpressionLandscapeLayerWeight.cpp:60-77`). Layer textures are
  culled per component this way. The weight comes from `StaticTerrainLayerWeight`, a static permutation per
  component (`HLSLMaterialTranslator.cpp:9030-9075`).
- `LandscapeLayerBlend` with `LB_HeightBlend` computes `w' = clamp(lerp(−1, 1, w) + h, 0.0001, 1)`, renormalised by
  `Σ w'` (`MaterialExpressionLandscapeLayerBlend.cpp:190-203`, `:254-258`). It also culls absent layers (`:178-179`).
  It has no contrast control.
- Megascans' surface blend exposes height per layer plus Blend Contrast, Falloff and Power (section 5 table).

### 7.2 Height blending with contrast

Mishkinis (Game Developer, 16 July 2013, "Advanced Terrain Texture Splatting"):
`m = max(hᵢ + aᵢ) − depth`, `bᵢ = max(hᵢ + aᵢ − m, 0)`, `out = Σ bᵢ Lᵢ / Σ bᵢ`, with depth 0.2 in the article. The max
is not a sum, so it cannot be built from `landscapeLayerWeight` terms. That loses per-component culling.

A form that keeps culling, derived here: `out = Σ wᵢ·e^{k hᵢ}·Lᵢ / Σ wᵢ·e^{k hᵢ}`. Each term is
`landscapeLayerWeight(base, float4(e^{k hᵢ}·Lᵢ, e^{k hᵢ}))`, so absent layers are compiled out, as in 7.1. The
relative weight of two layers is `(wₐ/w_b)·e^{k(hₐ−h_b)}`, a soft max on `h + ln(w)/k`. `k` is the contrast: large
`k` gives Mishkinis-like sharp edges, and small `k` gives a linear weight blend. `hᵢ` is the layer's tiled height,
in [0,1], times a per-layer height scale.

### 7.3 Distance and macro

Near and far scales of the same layer are blended by pixel depth (section 9.4). The macro albedo multiplies the
blended albedo after the layer blend.

## 8. Sand

- **Grain:** the beach sand measured by Nolet et al. 2014 has a mean grain of 324 µm and a median of 288 µm (PLoS ONE
  9(11) e112151, sample description). At 1024 texels/m one texel is about 1 mm, so grains are sub-texel. They read
  only through the normal and height micro-relief, roughness, and glints.
- **Ripples:** aeolian ripple wavelengths range "from the centimeter to the meter with a constant aspect ratio (≈4%)".
  Both the initial and final wavelengths grow linearly with wind speed (Andreotti, Claudin & Pouliquen, PRL 96,
  028001, 2006). The field runs show engraved wavelengths of 5, 13.5 and 30 cm, and an instability for λ ≳ 60 mm
  (Figs. 3-4). So ripples belong in the height/normal maps at 2 m tiles: `sand_03`, `damp_beach_sand` and `damp_sand`
  are described with ripples. Decimetre-to-metre ripples belong in the macro layer (`aerial_beach_01`, "wind-sculpted
  ripples", 30 m).
- **Wet versus dry:**
  - Nolet 2014 measured quartz beach sand from saturation (32% volumetric) to air-dry. The decrease of reflectance
    with moisture is non-linear at all wavelengths. At 400-700 nm "the slope ... is, after an initial steep decline,
    close to zero for intermediate moisture levels (5-25%)". So "only a distinction between a dry and a wet surface
    is practical". Visible colour barely changes: "soils darken when wet but with little apparent color change".
  - Zhang & Voss 2006 (Applied Optics 45(34)) measured albedos from BRDF. An ooid sand with a dry albedo of 0.6
    darkened only about 10% when wet. Natural sands with translucent quartz grains darkened significantly. A rough
    volcanic sand lost 35%, and a shiny black sand barely changed. Extra surface water "caused a strong specular peak
    especially at large illumination zenith angles". Neither the Twomey et al. 1986 nor the Lekner & Dorf 1988 model
    is sufficient on its own.
  - The rendering model in Lagarde 2013 ("Water drop 3b - Physically based wet surfaces"):
    - Porous albedo is scaled toward a factor `lerp(1, 0.2, porosity)` by wet level.
    - Gloss is moved toward water by the same factor at half strength.
    - Water F0 is 0.02 against 0.04 for common dielectrics, a change it calls too small to model.
    - It cites measured wet/dry factors of about 0.2-1.0 (Gu et al.) and 0.38-1.0 (Sun et al.).
- **So the wet band is:**
  - A sharp, nearly binary albedo drop over a narrow moisture transition.
  - A roughness drop.
  - At saturation, a water film with its own specular (low roughness, F0 0.02).
  - The water line comes from world Z against sea level (`SEA_LEVEL = 0`, `src/features/world/assets/coastShape.ts:2`).
    When the ocean work lands, it comes from the swash wetness field, which saturates while covered and dries over
    28 s in Tidewater (`docs/research/ocean-tidewater-level.md:169-190`).
  - The layer height decides where crests dry first.
- **Sparkle:**
  - UE ships glints as Substrate Slab inputs. `GlintValue` is "micro facet density ... 0 = only very sparse glints,
    1 = fully covered ... (=no glint)". `GlintUV` is "the parameterization of the surface required to position
    glints" (`Engine/Source/Runtime/Engine/Public/Materials/MaterialExpressionSubstrate.h:376-386`).
  - The implementation is Chermain et al. 2021, "Real-Time Geometric Glint Anti-Aliasing with Normal Map Filtering"
    (`Engine/Shaders/Private/Substrate/Glint/GlintThirdParty.ush:1-25`).
  - It needs `r.Substrate=1` (default 0, read-only; `Engine/Source/Runtime/RenderCore/Private/RenderUtils.cpp:2068-2072`)
    and `r.Substrate.Glints=1` (default 0 in code, `:2159-2163`; set to 1 in `Engine/Config/Windows/BaseWindowsEngine.ini:40`).
  - The kit's `unreal/Config/DefaultEngine.ini` does not set `r.Substrate`.
  - The TSX map already has `substrateSlabBSDF` with `glintValue` and `glintUV` inputs (`ue-material-jsx.json`).
  - Turning Substrate on is a project-wide switch, and it is the user's decision.

## 9. Recommendation

### 9.1 Textures (stage: content)

| Layer | Near set (real size) | Second set in the layer | Macro |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sand (dry) | `sand_03` (2.00 m) | `coast_sand_04` (3.94 m) shingle patches by height blend | `aerial_beach_01` (30 m) |
| Sand (wet band) | `damp_beach_sand` (2.00 m) | - | same macro |
| Shore | `coast_sand_04` (3.94 m) or `gravelly_sand` (2.48 m) | `dry_river_pebbles` (2.00 m) | `coast_sand_rocks_02` (15 m) |
| Ground | `grass_ground` (2.51 m) | `sparse_grass` (2.00 m) | `coast_land_rocks_01` (20 m) |
| Rock (landscape slopes) | `seaside_rock` (2.00 m) | `rock_wall_02` (2.00 m) | - |
| Cliff A / B | `rock_face_03` (2.70 m) / `seaside_rock` (2.00 m) or `dark_rock_02` (2.00 m) | - | - |

The wet band is not a fifth painted layer. It is the Sand layer's own second texture set, selected by wetness in
the shader (9.3). That keeps four weightmap layers.

The tile size is taken from the API's `dimensions` at build time, not typed by hand: `tile_cm = dimensions[0]/10`.
`polyhaven.mjs` also fetches `Displacement` (present for every set above). `coast-ground.mjs` packs it into the ARM
blue channel, which is metal and unused on ground, so the "ARH" texture holds AO, Roughness and Height. Height then
costs no extra sample.

The choices above come from Poly Haven's descriptions and categories. Visual acceptance in the coast lab comes after
the material is built.

### 9.2 Precompute (stage: tool)

A node step next to `coast-ground.mjs` writes, per set:

- the Gaussianized colour and ARH textures, using Burley's per-channel truncated-Gaussian transform (YCbCr luminance
  for colour when channels decorrelate badly);
- one row per channel of the inverse transform into a single LUT atlas texture shared by all layers.

Normals are not transformed (4.8).

### 9.3 Landscape material structure (stage: material)

One Custom node per layer, `<Layer>Surface`. Its inputs:

- texture objects (`textureObjectParameter`, `<Layer>Color`, `<Layer>Normal`, `<Layer>ARH`, plus the shared `Lut`);
- the world position;
- the pixel depth;
- for Sand, the wet set.

Its `additionalOutputs`: `Albedo` (Float3), `Deriv` (Float2), `Roughness`, `AO`, `Height` (Float1). The body:

```hlsl
float2 st = p.xy / TileCm;
float2 dx = ddx(st), dy = ddy(st);
SamplerState s = GetMaterialSharedSampler(ColorSampler, View.MaterialTextureBilinearWrapedSampler);
float w1, w2, w3; int2 v1, v2, v3;
TriangleGrid(w1, w2, w3, v1, v2, v3, st);
float2x2 r1 = LoadRot2x2(v1, Rot), r2 = LoadRot2x2(v2, Rot), r3 = LoadRot2x2(v3, Rot);
float2 s1 = mul(st - MakeCenST(v1), r1) + MakeCenST(v1) + hash(v1);
float2 s2 = mul(st - MakeCenST(v2), r2) + MakeCenST(v2) + hash(v2);
float2 s3 = mul(st - MakeCenST(v3), r3) + MakeCenST(v3) + hash(v3);
float4 a1 = ARH.SampleGrad(s, s1, mul(dx, r1), mul(dy, r1));
float4 a2 = ARH.SampleGrad(s, s2, mul(dx, r2), mul(dy, r2));
float4 a3 = ARH.SampleGrad(s, s3, mul(dx, r3), mul(dy, r3));
float3 W = float3(w1, w2, w3);
W = pow(W, Gamma) * lerp(1, float3(a1.b, a2.b, a3.b), Beta);
W /= dot(W, 1);
```

Then:

- Colour and ARH use the variance-preserving blend in Gaussian space with `W`, followed by the inverse LUT (4.1/4.2).
- The normal derivatives are rotated back (`mul(rᵢ, dᵢ)`) and blended linearly with the same `W` (4.8).
- The lattice helpers are Mikkelsen's Listings 1, 2, 5, 6 and 8. They go into one `additionalDefines`/include
  string shared by all layer nodes.
- One `W` per layer, driven by the layer's own height, keeps all maps of a tile aligned. Mikkelsen's φ (Eq. 1)
  allows any metric, and BaseMaterial uses "albedo or height".
- The near scale is the real tile.
- The far scale is the same node evaluated at `TileCm × FarScale`, blended by `saturate((depth − NearEnd)/FarRange)`.
  It is evaluated under `[branch]` only where its weight is non-zero. `dx`/`dy` are computed before the branch, so
  `SampleGrad` stays valid.

Sand wetness inside the Sand node:

- `wet = 1 − smoothstep(WetTop − Band, WetTop, p.z − SeaLevel − HeightScale·h)`. Crests dry first because `h` enters
  the threshold.
- The albedo moves to the wet set's albedo.
- Roughness moves toward the wet set's roughness.
- Above a saturation threshold, a film term lowers roughness further and sets specular for F0 0.02.
- `Specular = F0/0.08` in the legacy shading model, because UE converts the Specular input with
  `DielectricSpecularToF0 = 0.08·Specular` (`Engine/Shaders/Private/ShadingCommon.ush:122-125`). So F0 0.02 is
  `Specular = 0.25`.

Blend (7.2): each output channel gets a `landscapeLayerWeight` chain fed with
`float4(e^{k·hᵢ}·valueᵢ, e^{k·hᵢ})`, and one Custom node divides by `.a`. The normal output is
`normalize(float3(−d, 1))` after the derivative blend.

Macro:

- `albedo *= MacroColor(p.xy/MacroTileCm) / MacroMean`, with `MacroMean` written by the tool from the macro
  texture's mean.
- The aerial macro set of each layer is multiplied in under that layer's weight, so sand uses the beach macro and
  grass the land macro.
- The existing value-noise tone (`coast.material.tsx:37-51`) becomes a low-frequency hue/brightness term at
  ≥ 50 m, on top of the macro.

### 9.4 Cliff material structure (stage: material)

- **Projection.** Replace triplanar with biplanar (major and median axis, 2 projections), as in
  `MF_ResolveCoordinateFrame_Biplanar_Basic`. That is 2 hex-tiled projections instead of 3 plain ones.
- **Per projection, the 9.3 node body:**
  - It is hex-tiled at the rock's real size: 270 cm for `rock_face_03`, 200 cm for `seaside_rock` /
    `dark_rock_02`.
  - It has a derivative normal with per-tile rotation.
  - Each projection's derivative is turned into a world normal by the existing whiteout combination
    (`cliff.material.tsx:76-80`).
- **Rock A/B** is height-blended with the 7.2 soft max on `frac(v.g…)` bands. Rock B is evaluated under `[branch]`
  only where `useB > 0`.
- **Macro scale:** the same set at 4-6× its tile, as a second pair of biplanar samples blended by depth.
- **Wet line:** the existing vertex-colour wet term stays and gets the same albedo/roughness/film treatment as sand.

### 9.5 Sampler and texture budget

- **Sampler states.** Through `GetMaterialSharedSampler`:
  - one wrap sampler, `View.MaterialTextureBilinearWrapedSampler`, for all material textures;
  - one clamp sampler for the LUT atlas;
  - the landscape weightmap sampler.

  That is 3 sampler states, whatever the number of textures.
- **Texture SRVs (landscape).** Per layer: Color, Normal, ARH (Sand adds 3 for the wet set); plus 1 LUT atlas, 2
  macro textures and 1 weightmap or weightmap array. That is 4×3 + 3 + 1 + 2 + 1 = 19.
- **Samples per pixel per present layer:**
  - near: 3 maps × 3 hex taps = 9, plus LUT fetches (1 per channel transformed, or 1 for luminance-only);
  - far: the same again inside the transition band only;
  - macro: 1.

  Layers not painted on a component cost nothing (7.1).

### 9.6 Kit changes these stages need

1. `polyhaven.mjs`: fetch `Displacement` and read `dimensions` from `/info/<id>`.
2. `coast-ground.mjs`: pack ARH, run the Burley precompute, write the LUT atlas and the macro means.
3. TSX: a shared HLSL include string for the hex lattice, and texture-object inputs into Custom nodes. No
   `describe.ts` change is needed for this route.
4. If the engine's `LandscapeLayerBlend` or `BaseMaterial` functions are to be used instead of Custom HLSL,
   `describe.ts` has to link dynamic pins (`Layer X`, `Height X`, function inputs). The builder side already finds
   them by name (`VgeMaterialBuilder.cpp:342-354`).
5. Glints: `r.Substrate=1` in `DefaultEngine.ini` and a `substrateSlabBSDF` root for the ground. This is a
   project-wide decision for the user.
